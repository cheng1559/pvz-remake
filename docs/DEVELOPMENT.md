# Development

Adventure 1-6 through 1-10 follows the reference `Challenge.h`, `Board::PickZombieWaves`, `Challenge::UpdateConveyorBelt`, and `LawnApp::GetAwardSeedForLevel`: 10/20/10/20/20 waves with a flag every ten waves, pole vaulters introduced in 1-6, and bucketheads (without pole vaulters) in 1-8. Levels 1-6 and 1-7 take every available seed (five and six, which fit the bank); 1-8 and 1-9 choose six from the shared catalog. The available seeds are the ones the player owns (`LawnApp::HasSeedType`): the profile keeps them in `seeds`, a won level adds its award seed and a store purchase of kind `seed` adds the plant it sells, and the gameplay settings' `seedBank.startingSeeds` (the Peashooter) are owned from the start. The level gets the list as `ownedSeeds` in the start packet and keeps it in its save; without a player every catalog seed is available. The chooser, and an open chooser in a save, accept only those, while a confirmed bank may hold any installed seed (`/seed`); an award's packet shows the seed's own cost.

Rewards are Snow Pea, Chomper, Repeater, the zombie note, and Puff-shroom. The note uses its own dropped image, in-place fade, 500-tick black transition, paper sound and `pageImages` layout. Level 1-10 uses the existing conveyor with seven free plant types, no sky sun, a first wave at tick 100 and first supply at tick 200. Duplicate/repeated supply weights and the last supplied seed survive saves. The Puff-shroom award now leads into 2-1. The shovel follows bank width (`Board::GetSeedBankExtraWidth`: x=456 for up to six slots, x=609 for ten) and stays hidden during seed choice. Preview zombie tooltips reuse the shared button/tooltip controls and localized presentation `nameKey`, confined to the preview area. Once the player has the almanac (`unlocks.almanac`), a preview zombie listed in it (matched by `nameKey`) adds "click to view" to its tooltip and, clicked, plays the tap and opens the zombie page on its entry over the paused level; Close returns to the chooser (SeedChooserScreen::MouseDown, AlmanacDialog::ShowZombie). The chooser also shows the original Almanac and Shop buttons at (560, 572) and (680, 572) once the almanac and store are unlocked; the store opened from it says "Go back".

The chooser grid is shared across levels. Every seed has a `position`, its place in its group (`shared/content/seedGroups.ts`, 8 columns in reading order from 0, 48 places a page), unique in the group. The groups come in this order: the original seeds, each Mod's, then the hidden cards. The base's seeds are the original group, the chooser grid (`SeedChooserScreen::GetSeedPositionInChooser`): their places are the original's plant seed types, plants only, 0-47, five rows (0-39) until a seed at 40-47 (the upgrade plants) is offered, then six rows 70 apart from y=123 (`SeedChooserScreen::Has7Rows`); 48 is the Imitater's. A place nobody takes stays an empty silhouette. Base seeds marked `"hidden": true` are the hidden cards: explode-o-nut, the two bowling nuts, every zombie seed, the grave seed and the crater seed. A Mod's seeds are its own group, numbered from 0, never in the grid nor hidden; Mods move anyone's seed by overriding its whole definition (`overrides.seeds`). A seed's `kind` says what it puts down: `plant` (with its `placement`), `zombie` (the original's `SEED_ZOMBIE_*`), `grave` or `crater`. A plant or zombie seed puts down the plant or zombie of its own ID unless its `id` names another, and a new kind of seed is one more definition with its own `kind`, leaving the others as they are. Every zombie has a zombie seed of its own ID (`pvz:normal` for the plain one): the plain, cone, pole vaulter, bucket, screen door and dancer ones cost what the original's do (50, 75, 75, 125, 100, 350), the flag, newspaper and backup dancer zombies, which the original has none for, 50, 75 and 100; they have no recharge and work on any level (through `/seed` or a level's `seedPackets`): they go into any cell of an active row, whatever stands there, 30 left of the cell (`Challenge::IZombiePlaceZombie`), and their packet draws the zombie at its presentation `packet` offset, scale and pose `clip` (the pole vaulter on `walk`, as the original's cache does). The grave seed `pvz:grave` (`"kind": "grave"`, 0 sun, no recharge; the original has none) puts a grave in any cell of an active row the way Grave Danger's come (`Challenge::GraveDangerSpawnGraveAt`): the plants there die and it rises from that tick; a cell that already has a grave refuses it (`/grave` still replaces one). It is the zombies' own; a packet of the plants' side gives the grave `team: "plants"`, and the zombies rising from it after the last wave are on that side too. The crater seed `pvz:crater` (`"kind": "crater"`, its crater's `craterTicks` 18000; the original has none) puts a crater in any cell of an active row but one with a crater or a grave, the plants there dying; it is the plants' own (`seedTeam`), a packet of the zombies' side turning the crater's picture round. A grave seed keeps off a crater, as `/grave` and a level's graves do (`Board::CanAddGraveStoneAt`). A pole vaulter facing right vaults right, the jump mirrored across both widths; one put down on a plant's cell does not vault back. The grid holds plant seeds only. A level's `conveyor` gives a belt and `seedPackets` a fixed bank; otherwise the bank follows the original (`Board::InitLevel`): it takes every available seed when they fit, and the chooser opens when there are more. Slots are `settings.seedBank.baseSlots` (6) plus the seed-slot upgrades bought, capped at `maxSlots` (10) and at the available seeds (`Board::GetNumSeedsInBank`); levels list no slot count or candidate pool. The client builds `maxSlots` packets up front, so a slot bought in the intro only shows one more. `server/seeds/seedChooser.ts` supplies the grid, validates choices and confirmation, and saves their order and initial cooldowns. A bank lists its slots in order and an empty one is `null`: while choosing, the chosen packets come one after another (taking one out moves the rest up); `finishSeedChoice` starts the level and leaves the unchosen slots empty, which only a Mod's Let's Rock taking fewer cards does, and a short bank from an older save gets its empty slots back on loading. An empty slot draws nothing and cannot be picked; `/seed` fills or empties any slot. `/repick` (`startRepick`) chooses the bank again in play, its cards still chosen (closed up over empty slots), with Survival's repick recharges (`CutScene::IsSurvivalRepick`): the bank keeps `repick` (its size, whether the level had opened the chooser, and the packets it held) while `choosing`, the server runs no gameplay ticks and refuses everything but the choice (`choosing-seeds`), a card chosen again takes its old packet's recharge back and any other is ready (`SeedPacket::SetPacketType`), and no chooser warning is given. After a repick's Let's Rock, `finishSeedChoice` marks it `confirmed` and the level keeps standing still until the client, its chooser lifted out and the bank back, sends `finishRepick`, which puts the bank back to the level's own, chosen or dealt. A repick is never saved (`SeedRepick` keeps the bank from before it): a save made during one holds that bank, or once confirmed the bank chosen, with the chooser state the level had, and loads into play. The client part holds none either: its frame is the server's world as the save holds it (`IntegratedGameServer.savedSnapshot`, from the same `savedSeedBank` as the save), so loading shows no chooser. The client drops the chooser in and lifts it out on its own ticks (`LevelSeedChooser.repickTick`) without moving the camera, moves the bank to its choosing place at x=0 as the chooser drops and back to 10 as it lifts (`SEED_BANK_OFFSET_X`, `SEED_BANK_OFFSET_X_END`; 25 ticks each way, on the original's slide times, `SEED_CHOOSER_SLIDE` in `LevelSeedChooser`), the chooser's cards resting in the bank riding with it (`setBankShift`), lifts the bank into the HUD right under the chooser meanwhile, the level award's layer under the bank (over the lawn's entities, the coins and the award, which would otherwise come between the bank and the chooser's cards in it; over the chooser it would hide them), then puts it back under the entities; Space leaves a repick alone as it does the intro's chooser, while the menu opens (`LevelController.isRepicking`), hides the Almanac and Shop buttons and the shovel, pauses the music (`LevelAudioPresenter.holdMusic`, which the pause menu's resume leaves paused), stands the lawn's animations still, and builds a chooser the first time a level that opened none needs one; Mods wrap `LevelView.attachSeedChooser` to add to every chooser, and take the mouse wheel through `LevelSeedChooser.onWheel` (the level root listens for it): a node of their own listening for any mouse event would take every press over it (`NodeEventProcessor._handleMouseDown`), such as the Menu button's. Chooser cards and their pictures draw in reading order, each over the one before, as `SeedChooserScreen::Draw` and `SeedBank::Draw` go left to right. `LevelSeedChooser` places each seed at its position, leaving locked ones' places empty, rather than by preview image indices and handles presentation using the shared clock. Use `/level pvz:seed-chooser-test` to test.

The main scene mounts `app/GameApp.ts`, which coordinates the menu shell and starts `LocalIntegratedGame` through `app/LevelController.ts`. Gameplay runs in `server`; the client consumes protocol snapshots and events. Keep engine imports out of `server` and `shared`.

Implemented entities share their Almanac animation, body coordinates, scale and shadow with the level presentation definitions. Seed chooser previews also use idle clip coordinates directly. The almanac's plant page is the chooser's grid (`AlmanacDialog::GetSeedPosition`): a plant's place is its seed's `position`, its name its presentation's `visual.nameKey`. Its zombie windows are the gameplay `almanac` table, by zombie ID: `{place, startingLevel, spawnedOnly?, afterAdventure?}`; a zombie's name is its presentation's `nameKey`. The presentation `almanac` table gives the ground each plant or zombie stands on (`ground`: `day`, `night`, `pool`, `nightpool`, `roof` or `ice`, the day's without an entry) and an upgrade plant's packet (`upgrade`).

Local gameplay replies use `copyServerPacket` to validate JSON safety and deep-copy in one traversal, followed by protocol structure validation. This avoids serializing and parsing every reply after its safety scan. Nested Mod state, extensions and events are copied and checked too, so client mutations cannot affect the server or other snapshots. Client requests, external packet parsing and save formats retain their existing rules.

The `WorldSnapshot` (`shared/protocol/snapshot.ts`) names every field it carries, and `validateWorldSnapshot` checks them. The base game's own state has fields of its own: `tools` (the game's tools, `owned` and `available` now), `conveyor` (the belt's `ticks`), `dialog` (Dave's line `index` and its Yes/No `question`) and `debug` (the debug rules, once a command has set one), each absent when the game has none. `extensions` carries only the Mods' registered data on entities (`entity.extensions`), by its ID, and the level rule's `snapshot`, by its kind, which the rule's client view parses. `server/session/snapshot.ts` lists the fields of each entity it shows (`plantSnapshot`, the zombies), so what the server adds to an entity stays on the server until the snapshot names it.

Music and sound effects use MP3 through the existing `tools/pvz/music/convert.py` and `tools/pvz/audio.py` pipeline. Music uses LAME VBR q2 and effects q4 at 44.1kHz. Dynamic music stems retain their leading silence, gain and loop/beat metadata. This reduces download/package size; decoded PCM memory does not shrink by the same ratio.

Adventure 2-1 reuses the shared level and chooser flow: a night board, 50 starting sun, six chosen packets and ten waves. First adventure plays Dave lines 201–207 before choosing; no sky-sun timer is created. Puff-shroom stores its original random planting offsets (x=-5…4, y=-3…2) in the entity position and uses a centered half-size night shadow. It costs zero sun, refreshes in 750 ticks, attacks within 230px beyond its attack origin and fires spores that expire before collision at age 75. It sleeps by day. The newspaper zombie is fixed in waves 6 and 10. Its newspaper absorbs 150 damage, then it gasps at 8fps before walking at 0.89–0.91, roaring only with its head on and at most ten headed zombies alive; chilling also slows the gasp. These entities use the existing shooting, damage and save systems.

Sun-shroom, Fume-shroom and Grave Buster are installed (unlocked from 2-2, 2-3 and 2-4) and the almanac uses their level animations. The Sun-shroom (25 sun) drops 15-sun small sun at half scale, grows after 12000 awake ticks through the 100-tick `anim_grow`, during which it does not produce, then drops normal sun; asleep by day it neither grows nor produces. The Fume-shroom (75 sun) fires every 136–150 ticks after a 50-tick windup, dealing 20 to every zombie within 340px of its attack origin in the row; damage passes the newspaper and still hits the body in full (`hitsShieldAndBody`), and the fume cloud appears 35 ticks into the shot. The Grave Buster (75 sun) can only be planted on a grave, with the original advice, and only on one of the other side (`cell-has-own-grave` otherwise, without advice): the plants' on the zombies' graves, a zombies' side one on the plants' side's; zombies can eat it, but the shovel cannot dig it. It lands for 47–70 ticks (`anim_land` at the body's random 10–15 fps), then eats for 400 ticks while sinking 30px and eating the stone from the top, and finally removes itself and its grave, dropping a loot piece (`DropLootPiece` at factor 12) only when it ate a grave and is on the plants' side; the original drops one even with no grave, which only `/plant` could reach. Sun-shroom growth and Grave Buster progress are plant ability states and are saved. A sun producer on the zombies' side (Sunflower, Sun-shroom) makes no sun: each time it would, it takes what that sun is worth (25; a small Sun-shroom's 15) from the player's bank, down to 0 (the `sunDrained` event), glowing as it would and flashing the sun counter red without the buzzer.

Graves occupy one cell in column 7, one in column 8 and two in column 9, with distinct random rows. Ordinary planting is rejected with the original advice; grave cells suppress placement previews and mobile guides. They rise with dirt particles and sound once the intro pans back to the lawn, delaying the mowers and Ready-Set-Plant by 100 ticks. Their layout, appearance, rise delay and pending spawn timer survive saves. Two hundred ticks after the final wave, each grave spawns one zombie picked by the level's `graveZombies` weights, with a 150-tick emergence; none rises once the award has dropped. `GraveView` draws the original stone/mound atlas and `ZombieView` clips the emerging body, by the presentation's `graves.zombie` (how deep it starts and its clip, the original's `Zombie::UpdateZombieRiseFromGrave`, `GetDrawPos` and `DrawReanim` values), which a resource pack may change. Restore synchronizes visible grave heights immediately, including paused saves. The award is Sun-shroom, leading to 2-2. Night music uses the same `MusicSystem`, reproducing scheme 2's next-order drum entry and melody crossfade while preserving the separate drum phase in presentation saves.

## Plants and zombies

Gameplay authoring tables live under `mods/pvz-base/gameplay/definitions/`, split into plants, zombies, seeds, boards, projectiles, items, achievements, tools, the store, its pages and the almanac. `gameplay/settings.json` contains the global defaults, and the root `content.json` indexes every table. Packing merges them into `assets/gameplay.json` using the same structure as Mod packages, adding no resource requests; headless tools and tests read the same source tables. Plant and zombie values match PvZ-Portable and the previous version. A plant has at most one attack or ability: `shooter` (the shooters; `lobber` and `seeker` are the thrown and homing attacks), `producer` (the Sunflower; with `growth` it grows, as the Sun-shroom does), `fuse` (the Cherry Bomb: a 3x3 burn after 100 ticks; with `freeze` in place of `explosion`, the Ice-shroom), `mine` (the Potato Mine: up after 1500 ticks, risen in 106, an 1800 blast that leaves no body), `chomper` (the Chomper: a 70-tick bite, then 4000 ticks digesting) or `graveBuster` (the Grave Buster); a shooter may also have a `fright` (the Scaredy-shroom). A plant with none of these only blocks (the Wall-nut); `deathExplosion` makes the Explode-o-nut go off when it is eaten or struck down, and `mindControl` makes an awake Hypno-shroom bring the zombie or plant that eats it over to its side. The `pvz:plant-abilities` system in `server/plants/abilities.ts` steps each plant's `Plant.ability` state; the clips each state plays, the blinks and the Potato Mine's light are the plant's animation, which the server steps by `shared/content/plantAnimation`, and the views under `client/plants` draw it from the snapshot, `WallNutView` adding the nut's cracks.

Zombies have the original's two armor slots: the helm (`mHelmType`: cone 370, bucket 1100, football helmet 1400) and the shield (`mShieldType`: newspaper 150, screen door 1100), each written `{type, health}`; a zombie may wear both. Damage follows `Zombie::TakeDamage`: the shield first, then the helm, then the body. A shield takes no chill and counts 0.2 towards a wave's health, and the Fume-shroom's fumes pass it to hit the body as well; a helm and a shield that run out send `zombieHelmDropped` and `zombieShieldDropped`, and a mowed zombie drops whatever it wears first (`Zombie::MowDown`). The gameplay `animation` gives each clip's frames, the walk's ground track and the frames that mark bites and the fall, with `from` taking another zombie's clips; `vault` describes the pole vaulter's run, jump and landing. A projectile's `chillTicks` halves a zombie's animation, walk and bites, as the original's `ApplyAnimRate` does. The Cherry Bomb's and the Explode-o-nut's blasts char zombies into `pvz:zombie_charred`; a headless zombie turns black and stands for 300 ticks instead. New combat animations are registered in the `exports` of `tools/pvz/anim-defs.json`; `npm run build:content -- --pak /path/to/main.pak` converts them into `mods/pvz-base/assets/` and repacks the built-in ZIP, leaving the authored gameplay and presentation tables as they are.

## Server level structure

`server/` keeps no files at its root: `session/` holds the game lifecycle (`IntegratedGameServer`, `IntegratedGameHost`, `session`, `commands`, `snapshot`, `persistence`, `save`, `savedLists`, `createModHost`, and the generated `hostModules`), `state/` the world (`World`), the state types and resources, event queue and RNG, `registry/` the Mod registries (`GameplayRegistry`, `EntityBehaviorRegistry`, `JsonComponents`), `level/` level flow (`level`, `levelAward`, `waves`, `graves`, `conveyor`, `mower`, `loot`, `sun`, `items`, `itemSpawn`, and `lawn` for looking up what is on the lawn), `rules/` the level rules, `seeds/` the seed bank, chooser, planting, cursor and tools, `combat/` projectiles, their motions and targets, explosions and collision, `achievements/` the achievements and their triggers, and `plants/`, `zombies/`, `tutorials/`, `debug/` and `menu/` their own areas. Mods get every module by its path under `assets/scripts` (`host.get('server/state/components')`), from the lists `npm run mod:hosts` generates; a module that moves changes its ID.

`server/level/level.ts` initializes shared level state and advances level progress; `state/levelState.ts` defines the item, wave and tutorial state and their resources. `level/sun.ts`, `level/items.ts`, `level/waves.ts` and `zombies/groans.ts` provide shared sky-sun, item, wave/award and zombie-groan logic; `rules/` holds the level rules (`registry`, and the built-in `adventure`, `bowling` and `whackAZombie`). The corresponding progress, wave, and groan Mod system IDs are `pvz:level-progress`, `pvz:waves`, and `pvz:zombie-groans`.

The world (`state/World.ts`) is typed lists, with no ECS components: `items`, `gridItems`, `plants`, `projectiles`, `mowers` and `zombies`, each an `EntityList` holding the objects themselves by id and iterating them in id order, beside the world's `resources`. An entity is its object in one of the lists: `createEntity` only allocates the id (ids are never reused), `insert` puts the object in and `destroyEntity` takes it out. A system's `commands` defer both to the end of its phase, and `commands.destroyEntity` hides the entity from every list at once (`markDestroy`). The server save (`session/save.ts`, schemaVersion 4) keeps one array per list, each entry the object's fields with its `id`, its `position` (none for a grid item) and its Mods' `extensions`; ids are unique across the lists and below `allocator.nextEntity`, and `session/savedLists.ts` parses and checks each list's entries. Older saves are refused.

A row's lawn mower (the original's `LawnMower`) waits at its row's end until it sets off: `startSession` creates one per active row (`Board::InitLawnMowers`, `createMower` in `level/mowers.ts`) at `MOWER_READY_X`, a `Mower` of the world's `mowers` (`level/Mower.ts`, its `typeId` the level's `lawnMower`). A zombie reaching it makes `Mower.setOff` (`LawnMower::StartMower`) take it off its row and launch a projectile of that type where it waited: a `MowingProjectile` (`combat/MowingProjectile.ts`, the projectile kind `mowing`) on an entity of its own, which the projectile system moves after the other projectiles (`Board::UpdateGameObjects` updates the lawn mowers last), flattening zombies and plants and slowing as it chews (`chompCounter`); a plant it flattens stays in the world's `plants`, its `state` `mowered` (out of play, which `plantsInPlay` in `level/lawn.ts` passes over), until its squash has played. The two are not one class: a waiting mower is no projectile, and a running one no mower. Once the level is won, each waiting mower, the lowest row first, turns into a gold coin where it waits every 40 ticks and is gone (`updateMowerCoins` in `loot.ts`, `Board::UpdateLevelEndSequence`); 2-1's savings cap counts the mowers waiting and running (`lawnMowerCount` in `level/lawn.ts`, `mLawnMowers.mSize`). `/lawnmower reset` puts a new one in a row without one waiting. The original's `MOWER_ROLLING_IN` is only for bonus mowers (`mBonusLawnMowersRemaining`); the intro's mowers are `MOWER_READY`, placed by `CutScene::UpdateIntro` from the cutscene's own time. Here the intro's time is the client's (the server runs no intro ticks and only waits for `completeIntro`), so the roll-in is the mower view's drawing of the entity by that time (`MowerView` below) and no server state. Nothing installed squishes a mower (`MOWER_SQUISHED`). The snapshot's `mowers` lists the waiting mowers (`MowerSnapshot`); a running one is among the `projectiles`. A save keeps a waiting mower in its `mowers` (`{id, position, typeId}`) and a running one in its `projectiles`, with its `chompCounter`; the client presentation save is schemaVersion 3 (its snapshots have `mowers`); older saves are refused.

Projectiles have kinds as plants and zombies do: a projectile definition's `kind` (`projectile` when omitted) is its server class (`ProjectileRegistry` in `combat/ProjectileRegistry.ts`) and the presentation's `view` of the same name its client view. Each is an object of the world's `projectiles`, its entity, with its `id` and `position` (saved as its fields); `launchProjectile` (`combat/Projectile.ts`) makes one of its type's kind and starts its motion (`Projectile.launch`). A `Projectile` takes its `damage` off what of the other side it hits (`canHit`, `strike`, a melon's splash too); a `MowingProjectile` crushes it. How either flies is its motion's (`motions.ts`). The content check (`ProjectileRegistry.validate`) has each type carry exactly the fields its kind takes (a `projectile` its `damage`, a `mowing` one none), a plant's `shooter.projectile` overrides none its projectile's kind lacks, and each level's `lawnMower` be of a kind that mows.

The tutorial scripts are registered by ID in `GameplayRegistry.tutorials` (`server/tutorials/index.ts`: the base game's through `installBuiltinTutorials`, a Mod's through `registry.tutorials.register(ownerId, id, script)`); a level's `tutorial` names one, checked when the content is validated, and the running game looks it up from the world (`levelTutorial`, `getActiveTutorial`), which routes seed selection, cancellation, planting, cooldown completion, and sun collection callbacks. `plantingTutorial.ts` and `sunflowerTutorial.ts` contain only their special guides. A level without a script leaves `tutorial` out and uses the shared flow; a base script's steps are `TUTORIAL_STEPS`, a Mod's are in its namespace. Ordinary levels configure rows, starting sun, seeds, waves, and awards in `mods/pvz-base/gameplay/levels.json`; they do not need numbered server files. Special tutorials register a script without adding level branches to the main server.

Tools are the gameplay `tools` table, by ID, each with the `kind` that says what it does (`shovel`: `tools.use` digs up the cell's plant, which the client lights under the pointer); a level's `tools` and a `tool` award must name one, and each needs its presentation `tools` look. Tools come from awards: a tool one level awards is available from the next level on a first adventure, and on every level in replays. Flow that belongs to one level (Dave's dialogue, the shovel lesson's starting plants, 2-1's first coin and savings cap, chooser warnings, the seed-slot offer) lives in that level's script under `server/tutorials`, and the level's `tutorial` only names the script. A script's `dave` brings Crazy Dave, and `dialogQuestion` marks the current line as a Yes/No question the client answers with `answerDialog`.

A level's `plantingLine: {column}` is its red line (Wall-nut Bowling's): the player plants nothing right of that column, with `ADVICE_NOT_PASSED_LINE` the first time (a check of the shared planting decision below); the level presentation's `plantingLine` (`{sprite, offset, dialogs}`) draws it, in play and during the listed Dave lines. Any level can have one, with or without a rule. A bowled nut's rolling sound when put down (`SOUND_BOWLING` in `Board::MouseDownWithPlant`) is the nut's own `plantedSounds`. Waves are either listed (`waves: [{ zombies, flagWave?, points? }]`, as `mods/example` does) or built by a registered level rule, `rules: { kind, count, ...settings }`; a level has one or the other. Besides the waves, a level rule (`LevelRuleDefinition` in `server/rules/registry.ts`) may change the play where the original's Board calls into Challenge: `systems`, `spawnsZombies`, `initialize`, `boardClick`, `cursorCleared`, `dropsRefusedSeeds`, `canPlantAt`, `dropLoot` and `holdsAward`. Where a seed may go is one decision for both sides, `plantingRefusal` in `shared/content/planting.ts` (`Board::CanPlantAt`), over a `PlantingLawn` each side builds from its own state (the server's World; the client's snapshot, `snapshotLawn`): the server answers each refusal with its rejection and advice, the planting preview only shows whether there is one. The cell rules under it are shared the same way: `holdsCell` (a rolling nut and a plant a mower is crushing hold no cell), `canShovel` (the shovel and its highlight) and `graveAt`. A rule's `canPlantAt(lawn, seed, row, column)` is that file's `LevelPlantingRule`, asked for plant seeds before the planting line: the rule writes it once and gives the same function to its client view (`LevelRuleView` extends it), which the preview asks. It keeps its state in `wave.ruleState` (checked by `parseState`) and shows the client `extensions[kind]`, which its client view parses. Waves are announced by `nextWaveComing` in `server/level/waves.ts` (`Board::NextWaveComing`): the wave system calls it 5 ticks before a wave and as a huge wave's countdown ends, and a rule that raises its own zombies calls it from its own countdown of the waves' `nextWaveAtTick`. It shows the final wave's banner before the last wave and sounds the awooga before the first, otherwise the siren where its caller says (a flag wave); `wave.announcedWave` keeps it to once a wave across saves, and the client sounds it in `LevelAudioPresenter` (the `nextWaveComing` event). A huge wave's message is the level's advice (`AdviceWidget`, style `huge-wave`, as `MessageWidget` has `MESSAGE_STYLE_HUGE_WAVE`): it comes and goes by the original's TextFadeOn (`pvz:textfadeon_enter`, `pvz:textfadeon_leave`), and advice given while it is up waits for it to leave (`MessageWidget::SetLabel`, `ClearLabel`). The generic code only calls these hooks and never checks a level or rule ID. The client side is a view in `client/level/rules/` (`registerLevelRuleView`) with its settings in `presentation/definitions/rules.json`. The built-in rules are `pvz:adventure`, `pvz:bowling` (the adventure waves at four times the points, no money) and `pvz:whack-a-zombie`. The built-in `pvz:adventure` follows `Board::PickZombieWaves` for a first adventure: wave points, flag waves with their normal and flag zombies, an optional `pointsScale` (3 on 1-10), `fixedZombies` placed before the random picks (the zombie a level introduces, in wave count/2+1 and the final wave), and a final wave that gets each weighted `zombiePool` type it still lacks, in pool order (`PutInMissingZombies`). The rule checks its own settings. `zombiePool` entries are zombie IDs that take `points`, `weight` and `firstWave` from the zombie's `wave`, or objects that override them. The plan is drafted when the game starts and saved with it. Mods add a kind with `GameplayRegistry.levelRules.register(owner, kind, { validate, draft, ...hooks })`.

## Level presentation

`client/level/LevelScene` owns the common intro → gameplay → award/game-over lifecycle, game-clock scaling, input suspension and presentation saves. It depends on the `LevelGame` client API. `LevelView` composes the HUD, input, entities, particles, audio and end presenters; `LevelSeedBank` creates and updates each configured packet independently. Board geometry, active rows, seed costs and the level number come from installed gameplay definitions.

`LevelGameOverPresenter` uses the first 150 failure ticks to visibly move the winning zombie from its current row to the doorway, then continues the house-entry walk and camera pan. The approach stays in front of the doorway frame and outside its clipping layer. Loss positions behind the doorway edge, such as the pole vaulter's, move to that edge during the approach before entering the clipping layer. `ZombieView.syncGameOverWalk` moves the winner and its shadow into the layer of each phase, shows both, and advances the walk cycle through both phases without restarting it; the server snapshot remains frozen. Other animations pause, while the flag, surprise mark and other entries in the winner's `EntityAttachments` list (the original `mAttachmentID`) keep playing. Plant and zombie views register attachments with `attachments.add(node, type?)` from their context, and can remove one kind, as death does with the surprise mark. Players attached to a body track (`attachToTrack`) take the body's colors and hit flash, like `PropogateColorToAttachments`; list attachments do not. The failure sequence still lasts 1100 ticks with its existing sound cues.

`LevelPresentation` selects the intro and tutorial text/guide configuration by the level's ID (the presentation `levels`). The adventure intro (the grass roll, preview zombies and the timing of the mowers rolling in) lives in `level/adventure/AdventureIntro`; the shared scene contains no intro timings. Board backgrounds, music and game-over geometry live in the presentation `boards` (`boards.json`). `mods/pvz-base/presentation/definitions/` splits plant/zombie animations, packet atlas positions, view selection, part tracks, shadows and dropped images, into category files. The root `mods/pvz-base/content.json` indexes these tables using the same format as Mod sources; packing merges them into `assets/presentation.json`. `shared/content/presentation.ts` owns the types and parser, alongside `shared/content/gameplay.ts`. Later levels add backend content and a presentation entry (or reuse an existing one); new environments implement their board presentation. They do not copy the scene, input, HUD or save controller. Adventure 1-1 through 2-6 is installed.

The level's entities draw in the original's order, `Board::MakeRenderOrder`: `row × 10000 + layer + offset`, lowest first. The layers are the constants in `shared/content/renderOrder.ts`, named and valued as the original's `RenderLayer` (`ground`, `graveStone`, `plant`, `zombie`, `projectile`, `lawnMower`, `particle`, `top`, ...); layers from `top` up are not per row. Plants take `5 − x + 800` (further left draws later), zombies 4, shadows lie on `ground` at 3 (under every row's plants and zombies), dropped heads, arms and helms one above their zombie, and other particles the original's layer or their entity's order plus its offset. Equal orders keep entities in creation order, with particles at an entity's order after it. Presentation data names layers (a plant's `layer`, an explosion's `layer`; rolling bowling nuts use `projectile`) instead of numbers.

Client saves write `level`. Intro-specific validation belongs to that intro implementation. The shared level layout is `assets/resources/ui/level.prefab`, registered as `pvz:level`; its Prefab and script UUIDs remain unchanged.

Menu data follows `screens → client/api/MenuApi → MenuTransport → server/menu/MenuService`. `shared/menu` defines request and response DTOs. The backend owns profile storage/name validation, store catalog/prices/availability/purchases, challenge progress, almanac definitions, achievement entries/unlocks, and persisted options. Views render API responses and submit IDs/actions; they never import repositories or write profiles. Price, current-user identity, stock and balance are rechecked by the backend at purchase confirmation. JSON round trips isolate client copies from backend state.

Store responses specify `highlight` (`none`, `seedPacket`, or `additive` with opacity) and optional `seedSlots`/`quantity` labels, which the server works out: the seed bank's slots once the upgrade is bought (`seedBank.baseSlots` and one a purchase, up to `maxSlots`), and how many a `stock` purchase sells at a time. The client renders these fields without inferring a product's appearance from its ID; catalog overrides preserve the upgrade packet and Imitater styles.

`app/LocalMenuApi.ts` connects this API to the existing in-process backend; `GameApp` injects Cocos storage at startup. Backend modules have no Cocos/client imports. Existing profile, options and per-user save keys remain compatible. Presentation assets, localized strings, layout, sound and actual fullscreen operations stay on the client. A network transport can implement the same `MenuTransport`; this worktree currently uses the local transport.

Adventure 1-1 through 2-8 is connected. The debug CLI is available in Debug and Release builds: press `/`, or double-tap the bottom-right corner in mobile mode. See [Debug CLI](DEBUG_CLI.md). The levels after 2-8 remain unimplemented. The main menu uses original image buttons and its Reanim entrance; Options has working settings and in-game actions. Almanac and Store have their own content and interactions; other game modes remain unimplemented. Refer to the workspace's `pvz-remake` when porting their behavior.

`StartupScreen` automatically plays the 200-tick PopCap fade/hold/fade, followed by the falling title, rolling grass bar, and flower/zombie-head effects at the five reference thresholds. Custom image buttons accept `imageBox: { x, y, width, height }` to separate image bounds from the hit area, and `hoverSound` for a hover cue. The main menu's bottom buttons use this to preserve their larger reference hit areas while keeping images at their natural size.

A button's action is `onClick`, run on release inside; with `clickOnPress`, a mouse or touch press runs it at once. `pressSound` plays on press and `clickSound` with the action. Garden switching uses `clickOnPress`. Add `tooltip: 'text'` or `tooltip: { text, font?, maxWidth?, gap?, offset? }` to a button for a Pico129 tooltip with the previous pale-yellow background and black border. `offset: [x, y]` adjusts right/down before edge clamping; the garden tooltip keeps the previous center x=599, top y=52. It follows actual hover, appears below the button, flips above when needed, wraps long text, and stays within the enclosing viewport/Canvas. `ui.tooltip(anchor, options)` also exposes `show()`/`hide()` for manual use; disabling or destroying the anchor hides/cleans up its popup.

`UIButton` follows the reference shared capture behavior. Left/right presses stay with the original button through dragging; other buttons cannot hover until release. Releasing inside clicks once; releasing outside cancels. Desktop uses mouse events and mobile mode uses touch events to avoid duplicate simulated-touch activation. `releaseToNormalOnPressOut` defaults to normal visuals for original presets; `image` retains highlight but removes the pressed offset. The cursor returns to default outside in both cases. `rightClickPressesVisual: false` retains right-click activation without pressed visuals.

`onHoverEnter` tracks actual entry independently of visual state and drives `hoverSound`. `UIHoverManager` owns topmost hits, modal/page scopes, and cursor updates. Closing a dialog restores hover from the remembered mouse position; disabling, destroying, or switching pages cancels capture. `refreshHoverOnEnable` defaults to true; main-menu buttons follow the reference with this automatic refresh disabled. Factories configure images, callbacks, and polygons before activating nodes to avoid premature hover during construction.

Help uses the `seed` return button, and the Almanac footer uses its dedicated index/close presets. These controls and the Almanac's View Plants/View Zombies buttons are wired to page navigation at their reference positions.

`polygon` uses local pixels from the button's top-left corner, with x right and y down, within its `width`/`height`. Three or more vertices enable polygon hit testing; otherwise the rectangle is used. Edges and concave polygons are supported, including node scaling and rotation. Hover, left/right mouse input, touch, and release outside share the same hit area; points outside pass through to controls below. Press offsets move only the visual.

Options preserves immediate volume updates, the 0.65 sound-effect scale, fullscreen submission on close, required 3D acceleration, and forced fullscreen on native mobile. Restart and return-to-menu actions require confirmation; returning saves the level. Settings retain the `pvz-remake:settings:options` storage key. Native Windows and macOS remember the window like the original's `PreferredX`/`PreferredY`: `Game.cpp` follows the normal (not fullscreen, maximized or minimized) window's position and size, writes them to `window.txt` in the writable path on exit, and reads them before creating the next window, falling back to the centred 800×600 window when the position is no longer on any display.

## Resources

`npm run build:content -- --pak /path/to/main.pak` extracts the PAK into a temporary directory and converts resources directly into `mods/pvz-base/assets/`, grouped as `images`, `fonts`, `audio`, `reanim`, `particles`, `music`, `json`, `text` and `prefabs`. It updates the explicit mappings in `content.json`, then invokes the ordinary Mod packer. Converted assets are ignored by Git; authored gameplay/presentation tables remain unchanged. Only the bootstrap scene, compiled layout prefab and shader remain engine-managed resources.

Place your game's `main.pak` and `PlantsVsZombies.exe` in `tools/`, then run from the project root:

```sh
npm run build:content -- --pak /path/to/main.pak
```

The pipeline requires Python 3.10+, Pillow, ffmpeg and libopenmpt. `npm run build:content -- --pak /path/to/main.pak --exe /path/to/PlantsVsZombies.exe` imports and publishes the base package; without `--pak` or `--exe` the sources default to `tools/main.pak` and `tools/PlantsVsZombies.exe`, and a missing file is an error. After editing existing Mod assets or definitions, use `npm run mod:pack -- pvz-base --publish assets/resources/base.zip` without reconversion.

App icons come from the original game icon, which is not kept in Git either. `npm run build:icons -- --ico /path/to/game.ico` writes the web icons (`game.ico`, `icon-256.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`) under `build-templates/web-mobile/` and the macOS, iOS, Android and Windows icons under `native/engine/`; all outputs are ignored by Git. Without `--ico` the source defaults to `tools/game.ico`. The icon is the `IDI_MAIN_ICON` group in the original `PlantsVsZombies.exe`; its 16×16 frame is the window title-bar flower.

The mouse cursors exist only in the original program, not in `main.pak`. Before converting, the importer extracts the big arrow (the program's cursor resource) and SexyAppFramework's pointing and dragging hands (32×32 `CreateCursor` masks) that the original `LawnApp::EnforceCursor` shows, finding them by content hash rather than resource IDs or file offsets. They are registered in `content.json` as the sprites `pvz:cursor_pointer`, `pvz:cursor_hand` and `pvz:cursor_dragging`, ship in the built-in ZIP, and resource packs may replace them by ID. `CursorManager` maps `default`, `pointer` and `grabbing` to these images at their original hotspots (scaled by `scale` for higher-density replacements): CSS cursors on the web, SDL colour cursors through the engine's `jsb.setCursorImage` on native Windows and macOS. Turning `/cursor` off, or images that fail to load, leave the system cursors.


Save loading still checks the base content ID, version, hash and gameplay Mods. The builtin ZIP uses the same `manifest.json` and `content.json` as Mods, with gameplay and presentation definitions referenced directly from the common index; its hash covers every default file and resource mapping. ### Testing guidelines

- Automated tests focus on core gameplay, save and restore, Mod loading and isolation, client-server boundaries, and essential input flows. Check observable behavior and necessary boundaries; reuse or extend existing cases and avoid duplicate coverage and assertions tied to implementation details.
- Use the minimum appropriate validation for simple, low-risk changes. Check animation, layout, and text details manually when appropriate; add automated coverage when the regression risk warrants it. Do not add separate cases for fixed artwork coordinates, colors, or playback rates, or read source text to assert a particular code structure.
- For routine changes, run the relevant tests and typecheck when needed. Run `npm run test:server` for cross-module changes or when full validation is requested. It includes the server typecheck and all `tests/**/*.test.ts` tests, so a separate `typecheck:server` run is unnecessary.
- For example, run one file with `npx tsx --tsconfig tsconfig.server.json --test tests/server/save-restore.test.ts`. When tests fail, retain valid checks, distinguish new failures from existing ones, and do not hide failures by deleting or skipping critical tests.
- Report automated tests, builds, and runtime checks separately. Verify visuals, interaction, or platform behavior in the relevant environment when needed; a successful native build does not establish device validation. Avoid browser automation unless visual or interaction validation is required.

Windows and Android build commands remain available; Web validation does not establish native compilation or device validation.

### Preview module mapping

The Cocos preview compiles each script to a SystemJS module that imports `__unresolved_N` placeholders, which `temp/programming/packer-driver/targets/preview/import-map.json` resolves per module. After editing a module many files import (such as `shared/protocol`), a module's compiled code and its mapping can fall out of step, and its imports silently come from another module. At run time this shows up as an unexplained `undefined`, such as `Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'name')`, while the Node tests pass.

```sh
npm run check:preview                # check, and touch the sources with stale mappings so the preview recompiles them
npm run check:preview -- --dry-run   # check only
```

The script (`tools/preview/check-imports.ts`) checks, for every name each module imports, that the source file its mapping points to exports it, and lists the stale imports. After touching, wait for the preview to recompile before reloading the page. Without a preview build there is nothing to check and it exits. In preview, an `Error` reaches `temp/logs/cocos.log` with its stack, and `IntegratedGameHost` logs the original server error, which shows where it failed.

### iOS profiling

iOS forbids JIT, so V8 runs as an interpreter and JS costs far more than on desktop; judge performance on a device. `PVZ_JS_PROFILE=1 npm run compile:ios:release` enables the V8 debugger port (6086) in a Release build without enabling `CC_DEBUG`. After installing, forward it with `pymobiledevice3 usbmux forward 16086 6086`, then run `npx tsx tools/profile/ios.ts http://127.0.0.1:16086 10 /tmp/pvz.cpuprofile` to sample and list the most expensive functions. Use `/level pvz:stress-test` with `/fps` on for high-load comparisons.

Animations and particles no longer use a node per image. `ReanimPlayer` copies an animation's images into shared atlas pages (`AnimationAtlas`) and writes vertices through one or two `KernelBatch` renderers per instance; only tracks requested through `getTrackNode` get a node, and the batches split there to keep the drawing order. Keyframe sampling, blends, attachment matrices and vertex writes are implemented once, in C++ under `native/engine/common/kernel/`: native platforms call it through `KernelBindings.cpp` (iOS's JavaScript engine has no JIT and therefore no WebAssembly), while the web and tests run the same source compiled to WebAssembly, wrapped by `assets/scripts/shared/kernel/`. After changing the C++, run `npm run build:kernel` (requires Emscripten) and commit the regenerated `binary.ts`; other builds do not need Emscripten. Particles use the single schemaVersion 3 format in `shared/content/particle.ts` and the `client/particle/ParticlePlayer` entry point. RNG, spawning, lifetime, fields, colors and transforms run in the shared C++ kernel, with one `KernelBatch` per particle system receiving packed quads directly. Multiple simulation ticks enter the kernel in one call and render only the last state. Saves retain seed and age for deterministic replay; destroying or restarting a player releases its kernel state. Empty systems that have exhausted a fixed launch cap defer their remaining ticks and skip empty rendering, preserving their original lifetime and save age.

On macOS with Xcode, `npm run compile:ios:release` exports current resources and calls `cocos make` to compile and package `build/ios/pvz-remake-mobile-unsigned.ipa` for self-signing. The arm64 iPhone Release build requires no developer team. Cocos checks ZIP integrity and writes a `.ipa.sha256` checksum file; the intermediate `.app` remains under `build/ios/proj/Release-iphoneos/`.

Debug builds keep the level probe on the global `pvzLevel`. See [the Mod guide](MODS.md) for Mod development and installation.

### Client presentation of plants and zombies

- `client/plants`: every plant is drawn by its gameplay `animation` (which clip each layer plays and how far into it), and `PlantView` draws it: `pose` shows the layers on the plant's current clips and times; on the board `sync` calls it from the server's snapshots, and off the board the view steps the animation itself by the same rules (`advance`) and then calls it. Each kind of plant (the gameplay `kind`, its server class in `server/plants`) has the view of the same name, named by its presentation's `view`, which adds what that kind shows: `ShooterView` the particle that follows a shot and the muzzle effect, `ProducerView` the glow before its sun, `SunShroomView` its shadow growing, `FusePlantView` a burning fuse's jitter and shaken tracks, `ChomperView` the prey's arm, `WallNutView` the cracks, `GraveBusterView` the sinking, and the empty `ScaredyShroomView`, `PotatoMineView`, `HypnoShroomView` and `BowlingNutView`, whose kind's events they hear. A view reads what only its kind's class shows (its `snapshotOwn`, the snapshot's `own`: a producer's `produceTicks`, a chomper's `phase` and `prey`) with `this.own(plant, field, check)`. A Mod's plant view extends `PlantView` (module `client/plants/PlantView`) and puts its own clips on a layer through `playable` and `shown`; it may be created off the board, so what belongs to the board goes in `onBoard`.
- Everything on the board is drawn by its view. Each kind has its registry of views, together in `ClientViews` (`content.views`; Mods use `host.get('client/views')`, with `plants`, `zombies`, `projectiles`, `gridItems`, `items` and `events`), chosen by the presentation's `view`, and Mods may register their own. The five kinds of view keep one contract (`EntityView` in `LevelStage.ts`):
  - A registry creates a view by its class (`registerView`), constructed with the context `{ node, content, the entity, stage?, draw? }` and then `initialize`d. On the board it has a `stage`, off it none; resources, the shadow image and the current level come from the `stage`.
  - On the board `sync(entity, world)` runs every frame and the view places its node itself; a new view is synced once with the snapshot of the moment as soon as it is made. Plants, zombies, grid items and projectiles can also be drawn off the board (`client/view/offBoard.ts`): created by the same registries with no `stage` in the context, drawn as the context has them from `initialize` on, never synced, and `destroy`ed when done.
  - What must survive a save a view keeps itself through `snapshot`/`restore`: a zombie's body and mower-driver animation, the sun's and coins' spin. Save entries are told apart by kind, `zombie` or `item`. Plant and zombie hit flashes are the board's (`stage.flashes`, `EntityFlashes`), saved with the renderer's state in the level presentation save's `flashes` (schemaVersion 5), not by each view.
  - A shadow node outside the view's own (zombies, projectiles, mowers) is its `shadowNode`, which the renderer puts on the ground layer. With `draw.shadow: false` neither a plant nor a zombie builds one.
- The views:
  - `PlantView.sync` places and faces the plant, squashes one a mower crushes, tints one mind-controlled purple and one chilled blue (its slower animation comes from the server) and lights it under the shovel, then calls `onBoard` to show the plant itself; subclasses and Mods add their on-board presentation in `onBoard` and call `this.particle` for particles.
  - `ZombieView` is the view of the `zombie` kind, which the other kinds' views (`FlagZombieView`, `PoleVaulterView`, `NewspaperView`, `DancerView`) and Mods' zombie views extend; its `typeId` and `attachments` (what walks into the house with the zombie) are public.
  - `client/projectiles/ProjectileView` draws a projectile's sprite or looping animation, its ground shadow and its trail, turned the way it flies; off the board it draws the sprite or animation alone, without a shadow.
  - `client/projectiles/MowerView` draws a row's waiting mower (`Mower`, `LawnMower::Draw`), made by the mowers' registry (`ClientViews.mowers`): the animation of the mowing projectile it sets off as, held on its first frame where the original's cached frame is, with one shadow on the ground layer like a running projectile's. During the intro its place follows the intro's progress (`LevelIntro.mowerArrival`, `CutScene::UpdateIntro`'s `TimeLawnMowerStart`/`TimeLawnMowerDuration`), eased from `MOWER_INTRO_X` (-80) to the entity's `MOWER_READY_X` (-21), hidden at progress 0. Set off, its entity is gone, and the projectile it launched is drawn by `MowingProjectileView` (the `mowing` view), its animation playing where it runs, as a mowing projectile a plant fires is.
  - `client/gridItems`: grid items (the original's `GridItem`) are server entities, listed by `typeId` in the snapshot's `gridItems`. `GraveView` draws a grave rising and being eaten, `CraterView` a crater wearing away; their draw order is the `layer`/`offset` of the presentation's `gridItems`, their corner its `cell` from the cell's, and a packet's picture of one its `packet`.
  - `client/items`: `ItemView` draws sun and coins, `AwardView` the level award; an item's presentation may name another view with `view`.
- What a server event shows and sounds is in the static handlers of the views it concerns (`PlantView.exploded`, `ZombieView.rose`, `ProjectileView.impact`, `ItemView.collected`, ...), registered by event type in `ClientViews.events` (`StageEventRegistry`); Mods add handlers for their own events or the base's with `register(type, handler, 'before' | 'after')`. Hit flashes are handlers registered the same way (`EntityFlashes.plantHit`, `produced`, `zombieHit` and `zombieBurned`, at `before`, ahead of the frame's drawing), each event handled once by them; a Mod flashing for its own event calls `stage.flashes.hitPlant`/`glow`/`hitZombie`/`burnZombie` in a `before` handler. Handlers depend only on the event's data and the `LevelStage`, not on a view existing yet, so a projectile that hits the moment it appears still splats. A collected level award (`AwardView.collected`) always plays the pickup sounds and leaves the picture to the award's own view (`AwardView.collect`, found by `stage.view(id, AwardView)`): the sparkle, the arrow or glow taken away, the award's effect added. The dirt and sound of planting are the board's (`Board::DoPlantingEffects`) and are registered here too. Handlers of `frame` hear every frame once its own events are (the disco music's end). `LevelAudioPresenter` keeps only the level's own sounds: the music, tools, waves, the victory music, achievements, the mowers' coin notes, a dropped seed and the not-enough-sun buzzer. Sound lists written in the presentation play through `SoundSystem.playSounds`.
- `LevelEntityRenderer` does four things: which entities exist, creating and destroying their views by the registries, the draw order, and handing server events to the registered handlers. It is the board as the views see it (`client/level/LevelStage`): the entity layer, resources, the current level, the flashes, particles at a draw order (`particle`), a node of its own in the draw order (`addScenery`), another entity's node (`node(id)`) and view (`view(id)`, or only one of a class, `view(id, PlantView)`). While `quiet` (the level's first frame, a load) views show their state without the effects of getting there, such as a rising grave's dirt.
- `client/zombies/ZombieView` draws a zombie's layers by its gameplay `animation`: `body` is the body, followed by what it carries (the flag zombie's `flag` layer, later a balloon's propeller); a layer whose clip names an `attachmentTrackId` hangs on that track of the layer under it and is drawn where that track is (the original's `Reanimation::DrawRenderGroup`). The layer code, `LayerPlayers` (`client/view/layerPlayback`), is shared by `PlantView` and `ZombieView`. `ZombieView` draws what any zombie does; what only some do is their own views', chosen by the presentation's `view` as a plant's is: `FlagZombieView` (`flag`: the arm that holds its flag while it carries it, `parts.flagArm`, shown before any zombie's parts by `ownParts`), `DancerView` (`dancer`: moonwalking turned round, the leader's spotlight, coming up into a troupe, the disco music and the dirt of one called up), `PoleVaulterView` (`polevaulter`: taking its walk at once as it lands, mowed as it lands, the vault's sounds) and `NewspaperView` (`newspaper`: its enraged clips and head, its roar), which override `turnedRound`, `rise`, `bodyBlend`, `mowedOnWalk`, `ownParts`, `putOnParts` and `clip`. A flag wave brings on the zombie the gameplay settings' `flagWave.zombie` names (the flag zombie, ZOMBIE_FLAG) after up to `maxPlain` (8) of its `plain` zombies (the normal zombie, which every adventure level's pool must have; `Board::PickZombieWaves`), and the street before an adventure level leaves that zombie out (`CutScene::PlaceStreetZombies`). A chill halves the body alone (`Zombie::ApplyAnimRate`), and what it carries keeps its own rate; a dropped flag is the server deleting the `flag` layer. The view also handles damaged parts, the chill's color, the flag and mowing; the shadow is written `shadow: {x, y, backwardsX?}`, as a plant's; a plant's or zombie's presentation may leave out `view` (`plant`/`zombie` by default). Numbers, movement and ability states stay with the server.
- `ClientAnimationRegistry` starts from the parsed presentation definitions, only fills in what a clip leaves out (`rateScale` 1) and freezes them; a clip's fields are checked as the presentation is read (`parsePresentationDefinitions`). Add new presentation to its definitions and its own view rather than type-ID branches in the generic level renderer.

Gameplay and presentation configuration use `GameplayDefinitions` and `PresentationDefinitions`. Both are loaded, validated, returned as objects, and injected into consumers. Both parsers accept only each object's own fields (`exact`/`fields` in `shared/validate.ts`) and return only what they read and checked, with one shared copy of the point, pair and range readers; `ClientAnimationRegistry` only fills in what a clip leaves out (`rateScale` 1) and freezes the clips. `loadIntegratedGameContent` returns `definitions` and `presentation`; UI owns its definitions through `UIAssets.presentation`. Animation registries, entity views and seed packets receive their definitions explicitly rather than consulting global configuration state.

`shared/content/DefinitionRegistry` registers base and Mod JSON through the existing generic `Registry`, validates references after registration, then freezes the result. Server entries register gameplay tables, levels, explicit level patches and whole-definition `overrides` (any existing ID, one Mod per ID, applied after every registration and before level patches); client entries register presentation tables and names. `PlantViewRegistry` creates built-in and Mod views from the same registry. UI and levels consume the same Mod presentation definitions, including new animation targets. See the [Mod capability example](../mods/example/README.md) and [registration API](MODS.md#开发与打包).

A zombie's `animation` is written like a plant's: `clips` (frames, `fps` as a rate or `[min, max]`, `start`, `hold`, plus `ground` for a clip it walks on and `marks` for its bites and its fall) and `layers` (`{body: walk}`). `ground` is the Reanim's `_ground` x, counted the way the walker faces: a zombie's as the Reanim has it, a plant's negated (its Reanim faces right). A zombie's walk has no `fps`, its speed paces it; a plant's clip may have both, as the bowled nut's roll (`wallnut_roll`) does, and the nut rolls on it by the same `groundStep` (`Reanimation::GetTrackVelocity`) a zombie walks by; `from` inside it (`"animation": {"from": "pvz:normal"}`) starts it as another definition's of the same table, and what it writes beside `from` adds to that. The presentation's `clips` hold the pictures of the same clip names (`walk`, `eat`, `death`...) and take `from` the same way (`shared/content/from.ts`). Snapshots carry `animations` per layer for both, stepped by `shared/content/animation`. `tongueChance` and `walk2Chance` select variants. Plant ability `targeting` configures reach and the key zombies know it by (`insetKey`): the inset their `targetingInsets` give it, and whether their own mechanic passes it over. Post-vault attacks use `vault.landedAttack`. Every zombie has the same states (`shared/content/zombieStates.ts`), whatever its kind: one in the air is `airborne`, out of reach of the lawn (no ground weapon targets it, a mower puffs it, fire removes it, it casts no shadow), taken into and out of it by its own mechanic alone (the pole vaulter's jump; the original's in-air phases). Each zombie is of a kind, the server's class of it, chosen by its gameplay definition's `kind` (`zombie` when omitted) as its view is by its presentation's `view`, one class to each view class: `Zombie` (`ZombieView`), `FlagZombie` (`flag`, `FlagZombieView`), `PoleVaulter` (`polevaulter`, `PoleVaulterView`), `Dancer` (`dancer`, `DancerView`) and `Newspaper` (`newspaper`, `NewspaperView`), in `server/zombies/` and registered in `ZombieRegistry` (`GameplayRegistry.zombies`). A kind's own definition fields go with it (`vault`, `dance`, `enrage`: a type has those its kind takes and none another's, checked as the content loads). Each zombie is an instance of its kind, an object of the world's `zombies` with its `id` and `position` (made by `ZombieFactoryResource`): its fields are all there is to it, every zombie's (`ZombieFields`) and its kind's own (a pole vaulter's `hasPole`, a dancer's `phase`, `phaseTicks`, `age`, `troupe`, `summonTicks` and `leader`, a newspaper zombie's `gaspTicks`), plain data alone (another zombie by its entity ID), and its save keeps them as they are; it is restored as its kind, which takes its own fields from the save and rejects any other (`Zombie.restore`, `restoreOwn`; the saved list's `restore`). Its type's definition is no field of it. The rest of the game asks the zombie what only its kind knows, knowing no kind: how it comes on (its constructor, `start`) and sets off walking (`pickSpeed`, `setOffClip`, `walkAgain`: a pole vaulter runs holding its pole, an enraged newspaper zombie on its own walk), what it does of its own each tick (`beforeTick`, `count`, `pauses`, `act`: a dancer's troupe's pace, its age and its dance, a newspaper zombie's gasp, a pole vaulter's vault), whether and with what it eats (`eats`, `attackRect`, `passesPlant`), how it stops eating, walks and walks into the house (`stopEating`, `walks`, `intoHouse`), what it lets go of as it loses its head, falls or is mowed (`dropCarried`: the flag zombie's flag, its `flag` layer, `Zombie::DropFlag`), what losing its shield and being mind-controlled do to it (`shieldDropped`, `changedSides`), what plants can do to it (`dodgesBite`, `Plant::UpdateChomper`; `evades`, by a targeting's key, `Plant::FindTargetZombie`: a pole vaulter holding its pole slips from a chomper's bite unless it stands frozen, and is passed over by the targetings its `vault.evades` lists, the potato mine's `pvz:mine`), and what it shows and saves of its own (`snapshot`, `validSaved`). What only its kind shows the client goes in its snapshot's `own` (`snapshotOwn`: a pole vaulter's `hasPole`, a dancer's `phase` and `age`, an enraged newspaper zombie's `enraged`), which the shared snapshot checks only as an object and the kind's view reads and checks (`ZombieView.own`), so the shared protocol names no kind's fields: each server class and its client view class share it. A Mod's kind extends one of these classes and is registered by the name its definitions' `kind` give. The mine's client distance calculation reads the gameplay attack and body rectangles.

Presentation `projectiles`, zombie `helm`, `shield` and `drops` define sprites, tracks, effects, offsets and sounds; sound `pitchRange` controls pitch randomization (negative ranges lower the pitch, as the newspaper rip's -2). An armor's `dropSounds` play with its `dropReanim` when it breaks, unless the hit bypasses the shield. `seedPackets` owns atlas cells and placements; `almanac` owns cords and motion parameters. Add definitions for new content rather than branching on concrete IDs in shared renderers. View factories, dedicated ability state machines and unit conversion constants remain code.

Gameplay `boards.*.bounds` defines horizontal limits and a zombie whose animation has a `flag` layer carries a flag, dropped as in `Zombie::DropFlag`. Presentation `boards` defines backgrounds, music and game-over geometry. Particle snapshots retain optional remaining lifetimes; packet flashes use `seedPackets.flash`, with no effect-ID branches on restore. The gameplay `store` table owns the shop's items by ID (the key of their count in the profile): each one's page and place (`page`, `place` 0-7), price or price tiers, Dave's line or lines, requirements, inventory rule and purchase effect; `storePages` the pages in their order and when each opens (`level`, `completed`, StoreScreen::IsPageShown); `settings.json`'s `zenGarden` the garden a bought marigold goes to. The presentation `store` table has each item's look (`sprite`, `offset`, `highlight`), and `storeDialogue` the screen's timing and Dave's chatter. The application builds the backend's catalog from the gameplay tables.

Mods use `register({ host, assets })`: `host.get()` returns host modules and registries; `host.around()` / `host.replace()` patch actual methods. Separate system, screen and animation enhancement facades have been removed. A Mod keeps its data on an entity as `entity.extensions[id]`, a JSON object under an id it registers with `GameplayRegistry.components.register(id, validate?)`; registered data is checked and copied into saves and the snapshot's `extensions`. Server rules take the game `Session` first and are exported as objects: `server/seeds/planting` (seed availability, selection, planting, plant creation, cell lookup), `server/seeds/tools`, `server/level/items` (pickups), `server/session/commands` (`execute`, including `mod` commands) and `server/session/snapshot` (`build`). `IntegratedGameServer` only starts or restores the session, queues commands and advances ticks; `server/session/persistence` converts between the world and saves. See [the Mod capability example](../mods/example/README.md) and [Mod documentation](MODS.md).
