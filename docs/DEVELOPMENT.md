# Development

Adventure 1-6 through 1-10 follows the reference `Challenge.h`, `Board::PickZombieWaves`, `Challenge::UpdateConveyorBelt`, and `LawnApp::GetAwardSeedForLevel`: 10/20/10/20/20 waves with a flag every ten waves, pole vaulters introduced in 1-6, and bucketheads (without pole vaulters) in 1-8. Levels 1-6 and 1-7 take every available seed (five and six, which fit the bank); 1-8 and 1-9 choose six from the shared catalog. The available seeds are the ones the player owns (`LawnApp::HasSeedType`): the profile keeps them in `seeds`, a won level adds its award seed and a store purchase of kind `seed` adds the plant it sells, and the gameplay settings' `seedBank.startingSeeds` (the Peashooter) are owned from the start. The level gets the list as `ownedSeeds` in the start packet and keeps it in its save; without a player every catalog seed is available. The chooser, and an open chooser in a save, accept only those, while a confirmed bank may hold any installed seed (`/seed`); an award's packet shows the seed's own cost.

Rewards are Snow Pea, Chomper, Repeater, the zombie note, and Puff-shroom. The note uses its own dropped image, in-place fade, 500-tick black transition, paper sound and `pageImages` layout. Level 1-10 uses the existing conveyor with seven free plant types, no sky sun, a first wave at tick 100 and first supply at tick 200. Duplicate/repeated supply weights and the last supplied seed survive saves. The Puff-shroom award now leads into 2-1. The shovel follows bank width (`Board::GetSeedBankExtraWidth`: x=456 for up to six slots, x=609 for ten) and stays hidden during seed choice. Preview zombie tooltips reuse the shared button/tooltip controls and localized presentation `nameKey`, confined to the preview area.

The chooser catalog is shared across levels. `gameplay/seed-catalog.json` is the ordered list of seed IDs in the shared catalog. It is separate from seed definitions; Mods append their own IDs through a top-level `seedCatalog` array in registered gameplay data. A level's `conveyor` gives a belt and `seedPackets` a fixed bank; otherwise the bank follows the original (`Board::InitLevel`): it takes every available seed when they fit, and the chooser opens when there are more. Slots are `settings.seedBank.baseSlots` (6) plus the seed-slot upgrades bought, capped at `maxSlots` (10) and at the available seeds (`Board::GetNumSeedsInBank`); levels list no slot count or candidate pool. The client builds `maxSlots` packets up front, so a slot bought in the intro only shows one more. `server/seeds/seedChooser.ts` supplies the catalog, validates choices and confirmation, and saves their order and initial cooldowns. `LevelSeedChooser` follows catalog order rather than preview image indices and handles presentation using the shared clock. Use `/level pvz:seed-chooser-test` to test.

The main scene mounts `app/GameApp.ts`, which coordinates the menu shell and starts `LocalIntegratedGame` through `app/LevelController.ts`. Gameplay runs in `server`; the client consumes protocol snapshots and events. Keep engine imports out of `server`, `shared`, and `ecs`.

Implemented entities share their Almanac animation, body coordinates, scale and shadow with the level presentation definitions. Seed chooser previews also use idle clip coordinates directly. The importer matches existing definitions by their body animation resource, leaving only Almanac layout, portrait and seed packet pose data in the display catalog. Type-specific drawing tables in `almanac.py` are fallbacks for display entries without authored animation definitions.

Local gameplay replies use `copyServerPacket` to validate JSON safety and deep-copy in one traversal, followed by protocol structure validation. This avoids serializing and parsing every reply after its safety scan. Nested Mod state, extensions and events are copied and checked too, so client mutations cannot affect the server or other snapshots. Client requests, external packet parsing and save formats retain their existing rules.

Music and sound effects use MP3 through the existing `tools/pvz/music/convert.py` and `tools/pvz/audio.py` pipeline. Music uses LAME VBR q2 and effects q4 at 44.1kHz. Dynamic music stems retain their leading silence, gain and loop/beat metadata. This reduces download/package size; decoded PCM memory does not shrink by the same ratio.

Adventure 2-5 is Whack-a-Zombie (`LawnApp::IsWhackAZombieLevel`). The level rule `rules: {kind: "pvz:whack-a-zombie", count: 8, ...}` sets the graves, the refill count, the first grave column, the wave length, the three zombies and the dropped sun, with 8 empty waves (the first wave's countdown is the level's usual `firstWaveTicks`), and `server/rules/whackAZombie.ts` raises the zombies as `Challenge::WhackAZombieSpawning` does: 9 graves from column 4 at the start (`WhackAZombiePlaceGraves`), the first wave after 200 ticks and the next every 2000, graves topped up to 5 100 ticks before each wave, groups of 1-3 (with cone and pail zombies by phase) rising for 50 ticks at speeds from 0.5 up to a wave-dependent cap while the next wave is over 300 ticks away, and a final group of 20 cone or pail zombies 300 ticks into the last wave after the siren. No award drops before the countdown ends, and the rule's `spawnsZombies` keeps the normal wave system out. The mallet is the generic `boardClick` command, which goes to the rule (`Challenge::MouseDown`, `MouseDownWhackAZombie`): the front zombie within 45 of 20 above the click; a helm takes 900 to the helm only (`helmOnly` in `damageZombie`), otherwise the zombie dies at once with its loot, which may be three suns instead (`Board::DropLootPiece`, rarer with more sun), and the cursor is cleared. An emptied cursor is the mallet again only until the award drops, after which the mallet is put away (`Challenge::ClearCursor`, the rule's `cursorCleared`; the snapshot's `extensions["pvz:whack-a-zombie"].hammer`): it disappears with the blow that kills the last zombie and cannot swing again. The level starts with no sun, a fixed potato mine, grave buster and cherry bomb bank that starts ready (`openingRecharge: false`; planting still recharges, `SeedPacket::WasPlanted`) and no shovel (`tools: []`); a seed placed where it cannot go (a plant, a grave, or no grave for the grave buster) is dropped with the drop sound instead of staying in hand (`Board::MouseDownWithPlant`, the `seedDropped` event). A first adventure opens with Dave's lines 401-406; every game then flashes the packets for 400 ticks, 1500 ticks after the first sun, until a seed is picked (`whackTutorial`). On the client, `client/level/rules/WhackAZombieView` draws the mallet cursor (`pvz:hammer` at 24 fps, restarting at 0.2 on a click) and hides the system cursor under it when nothing clickable is hovered (`Board::UpdateCursor`, `UIHoverManager.setIdleCursor`), the blows' sounds and POW, and brings the seed bank down 2 pixels a tick once there is sun after the intro left it above the screen (`hiddenSeedBank`); items take clicks 15 further around and 30 more below. These settings are `pvz:whack-a-zombie` in `presentation/definitions/rules.json`; the generic view reads its `graveRise` and `itemPadding`. The award is the Hypno-shroom, for now only an awardable seed (the `mushroom` view idles and sleeps); its ability comes with 2-6.

Coins follow `Board::DropLootPiece`: each zombie rolls 0–29999 once, when it loses its head or is mowed, burned or instantly killed, with chances scaled by its value (its `zombiePool` points, which default to the zombie's `wave.points`): silver (1 coin, shown as $10), gold (5) and diamond (100, gold until the first seed-slot upgrade). Nothing drops once the award has. Replays always drop coins; the first adventure from level 11 (2-1) on, without any per-level setting. 2-1's script adds `loot.firstCoinAfterWave`, which guarantees a first coin with a pickup arrow after wave 5, and `loot.savingsCap`, which stops drops that would bring the coins (including the remaining mowers' gold) to the upgrade price. Coins are `kind: "money"` items that spin once landed and fly to the bottom-left coin bank, which shows for 1000 ticks and fades, and also when the game is paused (not by the debug CLI). From 200 ticks after the award, each unused mower turns into a gold coin every 40 ticks from the bottom, with a rising note. The profile owns the purse: start and restore take the coins and seed-slot upgrades from it, and every change in the level (including `/money`) is written back at once. The `items` presentation table supplies item visuals, animations and sounds.

## Server level structure

`server/` keeps no files at its root: `session/` holds the game lifecycle (`IntegratedGameServer`, `IntegratedGameHost`, `session`, `commands`, `snapshot`, `persistence`, `save`, `createModHost`), `state/` the components, resources, event queue and RNG, `registry/` the Mod registries, `level/` level flow, `seeds/` the seed bank, planting and tools, `combat/` projectiles, explosions and collision, and `plants/`, `zombies/`, `tutorials/`, `debug/` and `menu/` their own areas. Mods reach modules by fixed names such as `host.get('server/components')`, independent of folders.

`server/level/level.ts` initializes shared level state and advances level progress; `state/levelState.ts` defines the runtime components and resources. `level/sun.ts`, `level/items.ts`, `level/waves.ts` and `zombies/groans.ts` provide shared sky-sun, item, wave/award and zombie-groan logic; `rules/` holds the level rules (`registry`, and the built-in `adventure`, `bowling` and `whackAZombie`). The corresponding progress, wave, and groan Mod system IDs are `pvz:level-progress`, `pvz:waves`, and `pvz:zombie-groans`.

`server/tutorials/index.ts` selects a script by the level definition's `tutorial` ID and routes seed selection, cancellation, planting, cooldown completion, and sun collection callbacks. `plantingTutorial.ts` and `sunflowerTutorial.ts` contain only their special guides. Levels without a tutorial script use the shared flow. Ordinary levels configure rows, starting sun, seeds, waves, and awards in `mods/pvz-base/gameplay/levels.json`; they do not need numbered server files. Special tutorials register a script without adding level branches to the main server.

Tools come from awards: a tool one level awards is available from the next level on a first adventure, and on every level in replays. Flow that belongs to one level (Dave's dialogue, the shovel lesson's starting plants, 2-1's first coin and savings cap, chooser warnings, the seed-slot offer) lives in that level's script under `server/tutorials`, and the level's `tutorial` only names the script. A script's `dave` brings Crazy Dave, and `dialogQuestion` marks the current line as a Yes/No question the client answers with `answerDialog`.

Waves are either listed (`waves: [{ zombies, flagWave?, points? }]`, as `mods/example` does) or built by a registered level rule, `rules: { kind, count, ...settings }`; a level has one or the other. Besides the waves, a level rule (`LevelRuleDefinition` in `server/rules/registry.ts`) may change the play where the original's Board calls into Challenge: `systems`, `spawnsZombies`, `initialize`, `boardClick`, `cursorCleared`, `dropsRefusedSeeds`, `canPlantAt`, `dropLoot` and `holdsAward`. It keeps its state in `wave.ruleState` (checked by `parseState`) and shows the client `extensions[kind]`. The generic code only calls these hooks and never checks a level or rule ID. The client side is a view in `client/level/rules/` (`registerLevelRuleView`) with its settings in `presentation/definitions/rules.json`. The built-in rules are `pvz:adventure`, `pvz:bowling` (the adventure waves at four times the points, the planting line, no money) and `pvz:whack-a-zombie`. The built-in `pvz:adventure` follows `Board::PickZombieWaves` for a first adventure: wave points, flag waves with their normal and flag zombies, an optional `pointsScale` (3 on 1-10), `fixedZombies` placed before the random picks (the zombie a level introduces, in wave count/2+1 and the final wave), and a final wave that gets each weighted `zombiePool` type it still lacks, in pool order (`PutInMissingZombies`). The rule checks its own settings. `zombiePool` entries are zombie IDs that take `points`, `weight` and `firstWave` from the zombie's `wave`, or objects that override them. The plan is drafted when the game starts and saved with it. Mods add a kind with `GameplayRegistry.levelRules.register(owner, kind, { validate, draft, ...hooks })`.

## Level presentation

`client/level/LevelScene` owns the common intro → gameplay → award/game-over lifecycle, game-clock scaling, input suspension and presentation saves. It depends on the `LevelGame` client API. `LevelView` composes the HUD, input, entities, particles, audio and end presenters; `LevelSeedBank` creates and updates each configured packet independently. Board geometry, active rows, seed costs and the level number come from installed gameplay definitions.

`LevelGameOverPresenter` uses the first 150 failure ticks to visibly move the winning zombie from its current row to the doorway, then continues the house-entry walk and camera pan. The approach stays in front of the doorway frame and outside its clipping layer. Loss positions behind the doorway edge, such as the pole vaulter's, move to that edge during the approach before entering the clipping layer. `ZombieView` advances the walk cycle through both phases without restarting it; the server snapshot remains frozen. Other animations pause, while the flag, surprise mark and other entries in the winner's `EntityAttachments` list (the original `mAttachmentID`) keep playing. Plant and zombie views register attachments with `attachments.add(node, type?)` from their context, and can remove one kind, as death does with the surprise mark. Players attached to a body track (`attachToTrack`) take the body's colors and hit flash, like `PropogateColorToAttachments`; list attachments do not. The failure sequence still lasts 1100 ticks with its existing sound cues.

`LevelPresentation` selects the intro and tutorial text/guide configuration using the backend tutorial ID. The accepted 1-1 grass-roll sequence, preview zombies and guide steps live in `level/adventure/Adventure11*`; the shared scene contains no 1-1 timings. `level/boards/DayBoard` owns the day background, music and doorway geometry. `mods/pvz-base/presentation/definitions/` splits plant/zombie animations, packet atlas positions, view selection, part tracks, shadows and dropped images, into category files. The root `mods/pvz-base/content.json` indexes these tables using the same format as Mod sources; packing merges them into `assets/presentation.json`. `shared/content/presentation.ts` owns the types and parser, alongside `shared/content/gameplay.ts`. Later levels add backend content and a presentation entry (or reuse an existing one); new environments implement their board presentation. They do not copy the scene, input, HUD or save controller. Adventure 1-1 through 2-5 is installed.

Client saves now write `level`; the decoder accepts the previous `adventure11` field. Intro-specific validation belongs to that intro implementation. The shared level layout is `assets/resources/ui/level.prefab`, registered as `pvz:level`; its Prefab and script UUIDs remain unchanged. `client/showcase` only contains the standalone content preview probe.

Menu data follows `screens → client/api/MenuApi → MenuTransport → server/menu/MenuService`. `shared/menu` defines request and response DTOs. The backend owns profile storage/name validation, store catalog/prices/availability/purchases, challenge progress, almanac definitions, achievement entries/unlocks, and persisted options. Views render API responses and submit IDs/actions; they never import repositories or write profiles. Price, current-user identity, stock and balance are rechecked by the backend at purchase confirmation. JSON round trips isolate client copies from backend state.

Store responses specify `highlight` (`none`, `seedPacket`, or `additive` with opacity) and optional `seedSlots`/`quantity` labels. The client renders these fields without inferring a product's appearance from its ID; catalog overrides preserve the upgrade packet and Imitater styles.

`app/LocalMenuApi.ts` connects this API to the existing in-process backend; `GameApp` injects Cocos storage at startup. Backend modules have no Cocos/client imports. Existing profile, options and per-user save keys remain compatible. Presentation assets, localized strings, layout, sound and actual fullscreen operations stay on the client. A network transport can implement the same `MenuTransport`; this worktree currently uses the local transport.

Only Adventure 1-1 is connected. The debug CLI is available in Debug and Release builds: press `/`, or double-tap the bottom-right corner in mobile mode. See [Debug CLI](DEBUG_CLI.md). Adventure 1-2 onward remains unimplemented. The main menu uses original image buttons and its Reanim entrance; Options has working settings and in-game actions. Almanac and Store have their own content and interactions; other game modes remain unimplemented. Refer to the workspace's `pvz-remake` when porting their behavior.

`StartupScreen` automatically plays the 200-tick PopCap fade/hold/fade, followed by the falling title, rolling grass bar, and flower/zombie-head effects at the five reference thresholds. Timing uses 100Hz ticks from the global clock; effects use the current `ReanimPlayer`. Progress tracks actual main-menu image/animation preparation. The prepared menu stays hidden and is reused when input starts its entrance. Once resources are ready, the bar follows button mouse rules; background presses, touches, or any key can also start. Returning to the title skips PopCap. Generate the new clips with `npm run build:content -- --pak /path/to/main.pak`; the scoped build updates the existing conversion outputs and repackages the builtin ZIP.

Temporary black title bars are removed. `MenuPages.heading` declares original title fonts, baselines, and colors, with text from `pvz:strings`; Store uses its original sign. `ChallengeScreen` draws the 20/20/11 Mini-games/Puzzle/Survival thumbnail buttons and profile trophy counts, reusing `UI.button` capture and visual states. Selecting an unimplemented mode shows an unavailable dialog. `MenuPageControls` supplies garden cycling/Store entry and the achievement return button. Awards use original button images while retaining Adventure 1-1 replay.

Release startup prepares all assets and animation sprite frames. The almanac and packet previews share the content registry from `EntityDisplay`; `ReanimRepository` shares prepared animation data and frames within each registry and retries failed preparation. Loaders read engine caches directly, and almanac pages wait for portrait/detail first poses before becoming visible. Animation nodes retain the existing page lifecycle.

Touch planting follows the previous `pvz-remake`: tap a seed packet to keep it selected, drag out to show the plant and row/column guide, then release to plant. Returning to the source packet or releasing at an invalid location cancels; tapping another ready packet switches plants. `LevelInputPresenter` captures one finger until release and discards the gesture on pause. Swipe collection samples every 24px, selection highlights reuse `SeedPacketView` mobile mode, and planting uses the existing backend commands.

`AlmanacScreen` provides the plant packets, static zombie thumbnails, selection, detail cards, costs, recharge labels, and colored bitmap descriptions. `AlmanacText` preserves the original markup and lays out text inside `UI.scroll`; the shared `UIScroll` and `ScrollGesture` reuse the previous `pvz-remake` TypeScript wheel, thumb-drag, and mobile touch scrolling, with inertia driven by the global clock. `StoreScreen` supplies the car, sign entrance, four shelves, hover descriptions, money, and the 0.5-second hatchback page turn, using the global clock. `server/menu/StoreCatalog` reads progress and purchase slots from the active profile for availability, prices, stock limits, and confirmed purchases; money uses the original units ($10 per stored coin). The API reloads the profile before charging and persists it on the backend. Plant/zombie detail animations and Crazy Dave animation are deferred; purchased item effects in gameplay belong to their corresponding systems.

The almanac entries (plant name keys, packet images and seed IDs, ground, each zombie's debut level and so on; a plant's cost and recharge are its seed definition's `sunCost` and `cooldownTicks`, as the original's almanac reads `gPlantDefs`) live in `mods/pvz-base/gameplay/almanac.json`, registered as `pvz:gameplay/almanac` and, like the store, loaded by the app and injected into the backend; `server/menu/almanacCatalog` only validates them and applies the unlock rules. The almanac follows the current profile: it lists only the plants whose seeds the player owns (the profile's `seeds` plus `seedBank.startingSeeds`, as for the chooser) and are installed, and leaves the others undrawn. A zombie shows from its `gZombieDefs` `mStartingLevel` (`ZombieIsShown`; backup dancers, the bobsled team and the imp only after that level), otherwise its window is blank and not clickable, and is described only after that level (`ZombieHasDescription`); before then the description box centers `NOT_ENCOUNTERED_YET`. A finished adventure shows every zombie. Not yet implemented: the original also describes a zombie defeated since the player was selected (`gZombieDefeated`), and draws the Yeti as a silhouette before it can spawn; here the Yeti stays hidden until then.

`AchievementScreen` reads the achievement definitions (the gameplay `achievements` table: the base game's 21 in the original order, which Mods may extend) and the active user's earned IDs once through `getAchievements()`. Triggers, granting and the award-page list are described in `ACHIEVEMENTS.zh-CN.md`. The view uses the previous two-column positions, 70px atlas cells at 0.8 scale, green titles, wrapped bitmap descriptions, and alpha 255/32 for earned/locked icons. Shared `UIScroll` provides the original 435px wheel step over 0.35 seconds, 230px More/Top motion, and touch inertia; animation follows the global clock. Arrow keys follow GOTY: each press sets the speed to 30, which drops by 1 every 10ms while moving that many pixels (about 435px per press); key repeats reset the speed instead of adding to it. The tiled underground background and return control move with the content. Scrolling never requests data or grants achievements.

`MainMenuScreen` composes the current `ReanimPlayer`; `MainMenuConfig` holds reference button images, track offsets, and polygon hit areas. The entrance plays `open → sign` and holds the final pose, with independent clouds, grass, and clickable flowers. Store, Almanac, and achievements retain their caller, so closing them does not replay the menu entrance. Help, challenges, gardens, title, and gameplay replace and destroy the old menu; returning creates a fresh instance and entrance. `MenuPages.retainPrevious` declares these lifetimes explicitly, including retaining a garden beneath Store. Options, the player list, quit confirmation, pause, game over, and the in-game options are not pages but menu dialogs (`client/dialogs/MenuDialog`), like the original's Dialogs: `GameApp.openDialog` opens one at a time over the current page or the paused level, which stays mounted and takes no input until it closes. Main menu buttons name a page or a dialog with the same `target`. The Adventure button reads the profile for its start/continue image and level digits, then plays the 4.5-second flash, zombie hand, and sounds before entering the existing Adventure 1-1. Zombatar currently shows an unavailable message.

`MenuScreen.prepare()` loads the next page before `ScreenTransition` commits it. Main menu/achievements use the reference 0.75-second vertical slide and double S curve, retaining the active menu offscreen. `PageFade` belongs to the page: ordinary awards reveal from white and Help from black over the reference 180 game ticks of 10ms each (1.8 seconds at normal speed). It starts on activation, disappears with its page, and only draws; pointer input and keyboard navigation remain available during the fade. Help's Enter/Space/Escape return to the menu. Only resource preparation, page commits, and achievement slides lock navigation. Other pages and dialogs switch immediately without an extra generic fade. Loading failures preserve the caller and history.

`core/GameClock.ts` provides the shared `gameClock` for transitions, menu timers, Reanim, particles, and the local combat frame entry. `startGameTween()` reuses Cocos Tween, applies live speed changes, and unsubscribes on completion or owner destruction. Scale frame deltas once at their entry; server ticks remain 0.01 seconds, while explicit `advance()` calls and save restoration use supplied game time. In Debug previews, call `pvzTime.setSpeed(2)` for double speed, `setSpeed(1)` to restore, or `setSpeed(0)` to freeze game time. Options pauses combat independently so menus and transitions remain usable. Audio playback rate remains unchanged. Planned battle rewind and stepping: see [tick rewind](TICK_REWIND.zh-CN.md) (Chinese).

## UI components

The in-level Menu is the original 117×46 stone button at (681, 10), shown after the entrance. It first cancels a held plant, then opens the existing game Options dialog for settings, confirmed restart, and saved return to the main menu. Space opens the separate `GAME PAUSED` dialog; Escape remains a pause shortcut. Resume/Enter/Space/Escape return to the same board, and holding Space cannot toggle repeatedly. Pause saves the current game and freezes server logic, scene Reanim/particles/tweens and music without changing the global clock. Only previously enabled animation components resume. The pause dialog reserves the reference header spacing for its deferred newspaper-zombie animation.

`client/ui/UI.ts` creates labels, buttons, inputs, checkboxes, selects, sliders, scroll areas, and dialogs. Coordinates are parent-local pixels, with x pointing right and y pointing down. Text uses the existing `FontRenderer` and original bitmap fonts. Controls reuse the reference textures and interactions. Programmatic `value` changes are silent; user input invokes `onChange`.

```ts
const ui = await UI.load()
const label = ui.label(parent, { text: 'Music', x: 20, y: 30 })
const button = ui.button(parent, { text: 'OK', x: 20, y: 80, onClick: close })
const slider = ui.slider(parent, { x: 220, y: 30, value: 0.85, onChange: setVolume })
const checkbox = ui.checkbox(parent, { x: 220, y: 80, value: true, onChange: setChecked })
const select = ui.select(parent, { x: 20, y: 140, items: ['A', 'B'], value: 0, onChange: selectItem })
const input = ui.input(parent, { x: 20, y: 190, width: 300, maxLength: 12, onSubmit: saveName })
const confirmed = await ui.dialog(parent, { title: 'Continue?', cancelText: 'CANCEL' }).result
```

`ui.scroll(parent, { x, y, width, height, contentHeight, wheelStep?, bottomPadding?, scrollbar?, wheelDuration? })` creates a vertical `UIScroll`. Add labels, images, or other nodes to `scroll.content`, using `scroll.contentWidth` for layout. Set `scroll.offset` to scroll in pixels, and call `scroll.setContentHeight(height)` after content changes. The component owns clipping, automatic scrollbar visibility, legacy thumb rendering/dragging, touch inertia, and listener cleanup; it uses the global clock. Almanac supplies its 0.6-line wheel step and bottom padding. `scrollbar: false` retains full content width; `wheelDuration` enables the previous double-S easing, and `scroll.scrollTo(offset, seconds)` animates programmatic movement.

Labels expose `text`, `color`, and `setPosition(x, y)`; buttons expose `setText()` and `interactable`; input controls expose `value`. Dialogs support modal input blocking, dragging, topmost Enter/Space/Escape handling, and `close()`.

`UI.label` accepts a font preset or loaded `BitmapFontAssets`, and `node` binds an existing prefab text node without adding a second renderer. `pixelAlign` rounds the final position for original HUD text. Adventure 1-1 uses these labels for sun, level, house name and advice; fonts still come from its content registry so Mod overrides apply. Seed packets use `UIButton` for press/capture/modal handling, with suspension disabling the component. Cooldown, selection, flashes, advice animation and the progress meter remain specialized rendering.

`UIInput` exposes `value`, `interactable`, `focus()`, `blur()`, `selectAll()`, and `onChange`/`onSubmit`/`onCancel`. `filter` sanitizes input and `maxLength` limits it. Cocos `EditBox` handles keyboard, IME, and clipboard input; original bitmap text, edit-box art, selection, and caret remain game-rendered. Web selection access stays inside the component. Pointer input positions/drags the caret or selects all on double click, with a text cursor. Focused typing Space does not confirm a dialog. Upper modals release focus; closing validation feedback restores it.

`dialogs/UserDialog.ts` calls `MenuApi` for selecting, creating, renaming, and deleting users; the backend validates and persists these operations. It shows up to eight users with the current user first; single click selects, double click/OK commits, and Cancel preserves the current user. Names follow the reference limit of 12 ASCII letters/digits/spaces, with collapsed spaces and empty/case-insensitive duplicate validation. Confirmed deletion removes only that user's legacy and primary/temp saves. First launch and deleting the last user require creating a user. The retained main menu refreshes its name and Adventure state. User dialogs reuse the shared tall-bottom frame, extra sizing, and top-aligned message layout.

Buttons select original presets through `skin`. Sizes default to the source image, except stone buttons (180×46):

| skin | Appearance |
| --- | --- |
| `stone` | Default tiled stone button with highlighted hover glyphs |
| `back` | Large Options return button, 360×100 |
| `seed` | Large seed chooser button, 156×42, additive glow and disabled image |
| `seedSmall` | Small seed chooser button, 111×26, hover image swap |

```ts
ui.button(parent, { skin: 'seed', text: 'Continue', x: 20, y: 80, onClick: next })
ui.button(parent, {
    x: 400, y: 100,
    images: { normal, hover, pressed, disabled }, // SpriteFrames; only normal is required
    polygon: [[7, 1], [328, 30], [314, 125], [1, 78]],
    onClick: startAdventure,
})
```

Load images with the existing `modResources.asset(id, SpriteFrame)`. Missing states fall back to the normal image; pressed first falls back to hover. Disabled buttons without a disabled image use half opacity. Override `font`, `color`, or `pressOffset: [x, y]` as needed. Set `pressSound` or `clickSound` to `null` to mute it, and `rightClickTriggers: false` to disable right-click activation. `rightClickPressesVisual: false` keeps right-click activation without a pressed visual, as used by the Almanac footer presets.

Custom image buttons accept `imageBox: { x, y, width, height }` to separate image bounds from the hit area, and `hoverSound` for a hover cue. The main menu's bottom buttons use this to preserve their larger reference hit areas while keeping images at their natural size.

A button's action is `onClick`, run on release inside; with `clickOnPress`, a mouse or touch press runs it at once. `pressSound` plays on press and `clickSound` with the action. Garden switching uses `clickOnPress`. Add `tooltip: 'text'` or `tooltip: { text, font?, maxWidth?, gap?, offset? }` to a button for a Pico129 tooltip with the previous pale-yellow background and black border. `offset: [x, y]` adjusts right/down before edge clamping; the garden tooltip keeps the previous center x=599, top y=52. It follows actual hover, appears below the button, flips above when needed, wraps long text, and stays within the enclosing viewport/Canvas. `ui.tooltip(anchor, options)` also exposes `show()`/`hide()` for manual use; disabling or destroying the anchor hides/cleans up its popup.

`UIButton` follows the reference shared capture behavior. Left/right presses stay with the original button through dragging; other buttons cannot hover until release. Releasing inside clicks once; releasing outside cancels. Desktop uses mouse events and mobile mode uses touch events to avoid duplicate simulated-touch activation. `releaseToNormalOnPressOut` defaults to normal visuals for original presets; `image` retains highlight but removes the pressed offset. The cursor returns to default outside in both cases. `rightClickPressesVisual: false` retains right-click activation without pressed visuals.

`onHoverEnter` tracks actual entry independently of visual state and drives `hoverSound`. `UIHoverManager` owns topmost hits, modal/page scopes, and cursor updates. Closing a dialog restores hover from the remembered mouse position; disabling, destroying, or switching pages cancels capture. `refreshHoverOnEnable` defaults to true; main-menu buttons follow the reference with this automatic refresh disabled. Factories configure images, callbacks, and polygons before activating nodes to avoid premature hover during construction.

Help uses the `seed` return button, and the Almanac footer uses its dedicated index/close presets. These controls and the Almanac's View Plants/View Zombies buttons are wired to page navigation at their reference positions.

`polygon` uses local pixels from the button's top-left corner, with x right and y down, within its `width`/`height`. Three or more vertices enable polygon hit testing; otherwise the rectangle is used. Edges and concave polygons are supported, including node scaling and rotation. Hover, left/right mouse input, touch, and release outside share the same hit area; points outside pass through to controls below. Press offsets move only the visual.

Options preserves immediate volume updates, the 0.65 sound-effect scale, fullscreen submission on close, required 3D acceleration, and forced fullscreen on native mobile. Restart and return-to-menu actions require confirmation; returning saves the level. Settings retain the `pvz-remake:settings:options` storage key. Native Windows and macOS remember the window like the original's `PreferredX`/`PreferredY`: `Game.cpp` follows the normal (not fullscreen, maximized or minimized) window's position and size, writes them to `window.txt` in the writable path on exit, and reads them before creating the next window, falling back to the centred 800×600 window when the position is no longer on any display.

## Resources

`npm run build:content -- --pak /path/to/main.pak` extracts the PAK into a temporary directory and converts resources directly into `mods/pvz-base/assets/`, grouped as `images`, `fonts`, `audio`, `reanim`, `particles`, `music`, `json`, `text` and `prefabs`. It updates the explicit mappings in `content.json`, then invokes the ordinary Mod packer. Converted assets are ignored by Git; authored gameplay/presentation tables remain unchanged. Only the bootstrap scene, compiled layout prefab and shader remain engine-managed resources.

Place your game's `main.pak` and `PlantsVsZombies.exe` in `tools/`, then run from the project root:

```sh
npm run build:content -- --pak /path/to/main.pak
```

The pipeline requires Python 3.10+, Pillow, ffmpeg and libopenmpt. `npm run build:content -- --pak /path/to/main.pak --exe /path/to/PlantsVsZombies.exe` imports and publishes the base package; without `--pak` or `--exe` the sources default to `tools/main.pak` and `tools/PlantsVsZombies.exe`, and a missing file is an error. After editing existing Mod assets or definitions, use `npm run mod:pack -- pvz-base --publish assets/resources/base.zip` without reconversion.

App icons come from the original game icon, which is not kept in Git either. `npm run build:icons -- --ico /path/to/game.ico` writes the web icons (`game.ico`, `icon-256.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`) under `build-templates/web-mobile/` and the macOS, iOS, Android and Windows icons under `native/engine/`; all outputs are ignored by Git. Without `--ico` the source defaults to `tools/game.ico`. The icon is the `IDI_MAIN_ICON` group in the original `PlantsVsZombies.exe`; its 16×16 frame is the window title-bar flower.

The mouse cursors exist only in the original program, not in `main.pak`. Before converting, the importer extracts the big arrow (the program's cursor resource) and SexyAppFramework's pointing and dragging hands (32×32 `CreateCursor` masks) that the original `LawnApp::EnforceCursor` shows, finding them by content hash rather than resource IDs or file offsets. They are registered in `content.json` as the sprites `pvz:cursor_pointer`, `pvz:cursor_hand` and `pvz:cursor_dragging`, ship in the built-in ZIP, and resource packs may replace them by ID. `CursorManager` maps `default`, `pointer` and `grabbing` to these images at their original hotspots (scaled by `scale` for higher-density replacements): CSS cursors on the web, SDL colour cursors through the engine's `jsb.setCursorImage` on native Windows and macOS. `/cursor false`, or images that fail to load, leave the system cursors.


Save loading still checks the base content ID, version, hash and gameplay Mods. The builtin ZIP uses the same `manifest.json` and `content.json` as Mods, with gameplay and presentation definitions referenced directly from the common index; its hash covers every default file and resource mapping. Presentation overrides do not modify authoritative gameplay JSON.

## Checks and builds

```sh
npm run typecheck:server
npm run test:server
npm run build:web:debug
```

### Testing guidelines

- Automated tests focus on core gameplay, save and restore, Mod loading and isolation, client-server boundaries, and essential input flows. Check observable behavior and necessary boundaries; reuse or extend existing cases and avoid duplicate coverage and assertions tied to implementation details.
- Use the minimum appropriate validation for simple, low-risk changes. Check animation, layout, and text details manually when appropriate; add automated coverage when the regression risk warrants it. Do not add separate cases for fixed artwork coordinates, colors, or playback rates, or read source text to assert a particular code structure.
- For routine changes, run the relevant tests and typecheck when needed. Run `npm run test:server` for cross-module changes or when full validation is requested. It includes the server typecheck and all `tests/**/*.test.ts` tests, so a separate `typecheck:server` run is unnecessary.
- For example, run one file with `npx tsx --tsconfig tsconfig.server.json --test tests/server/save-restore.test.ts`. When tests fail, retain valid checks, distinguish new failures from existing ones, and do not hide failures by deleting or skipping critical tests.
- Report automated tests, builds, and runtime checks separately. Verify visuals, interaction, or platform behavior in the relevant environment when needed; a successful native build does not establish device validation. Avoid browser automation unless visual or interaction validation is required.

Windows and Android build commands remain available; Web validation does not establish native compilation or device validation.

### iOS profiling

iOS forbids JIT, so V8 runs as an interpreter and JS costs far more than on desktop; judge performance on a device. `PVZ_JS_PROFILE=1 npm run compile:ios:release` enables the V8 debugger port (6086) in a Release build without enabling `CC_DEBUG`. After installing, forward it with `pymobiledevice3 usbmux forward 16086 6086`, then run `npx tsx tools/profile/ios.ts http://127.0.0.1:16086 10 /tmp/pvz.cpuprofile` to sample and list the most expensive functions. Use `/level pvz:stress-test` with `/fps true` for high-load comparisons.

Animations and particles no longer use a node per image. `ReanimPlayer` copies an animation's images into shared atlas pages (`AnimationAtlas`) and writes vertices through one or two `ReanimBatch` renderers per instance; only tracks requested through `getTrackNode` get a node, and the batches split there to keep the drawing order. Keyframe sampling, blends, attachment matrices and vertex writes are implemented once, in C++ under `native/engine/common/kernel/`: native platforms call it through `KernelBindings.cpp` (iOS's JavaScript engine has no JIT and therefore no WebAssembly), while the web and tests run the same source compiled to WebAssembly, wrapped by `assets/scripts/shared/kernel/`. After changing the C++, run `npm run build:kernel` (requires Emscripten) and commit the regenerated `binary.ts`; other builds do not need Emscripten. Particles use the single schemaVersion 3 format in `shared/content/particle.ts` and the `client/particle/ParticlePlayer` entry point. RNG, spawning, lifetime, fields, colors and transforms run in the shared C++ kernel, with one `ParticleBatch` per emitter receiving packed quads directly. Multiple simulation ticks enter the kernel in one call and render only the last state. Saves retain seed and age for deterministic replay; destroying or restarting a player releases its kernel state. Empty systems that have exhausted a fixed launch cap defer their remaining ticks and skip empty rendering, preserving their original lifetime and save age.

On macOS with Xcode, `npm run compile:ios:release` exports current resources and calls `cocos make` to compile and package `build/ios/pvz-remake-mobile-unsigned.ipa` for self-signing. The arm64 iPhone Release build requires no developer team. Cocos checks ZIP integrity and writes a `.ipa.sha256` checksum file; the intermediate `.app` remains under `build/ios/proj/Release-iphoneos/`.

The `pvzPhase4` level probe remains available in debug builds. See [the Mod guide](MODS.md) for Mod development and installation.

Gameplay and presentation configuration use `GameplayDefinitions` and `PresentationDefinitions`. Both are loaded, validated, returned as objects, and injected into consumers. `loadIntegratedGameContent` returns `definitions` and `presentation`; UI owns its definitions through `UIAssets.presentation`. Animation registries, entity views and seed packets receive their definitions explicitly rather than consulting global configuration state.

`shared/content/DefinitionRegistry` registers base and Mod JSON through the existing generic `Registry`, validates references after registration, then freezes the result. Server entries register gameplay tables, levels, explicit level patches and whole-definition `overrides` (any existing ID, one Mod per ID, applied after every registration and before level patches); client entries register presentation tables and names. `PlantViewRegistry` creates built-in and Mod views from the same registry. UI and levels consume the same Mod presentation definitions, including new animation targets. See the [Mod capability example](../mods/example/README.md) and [registration API](MODS.md#新增植物与内容注册).

Zombie `gait` references the gameplay `zombieGaits` table; `tongueChance` and `walk2Chance` explicitly select variants independently of the gait. Plant ability `targeting` configures reach, held-pole filtering and an interaction inset key resolved through zombie `targetingInsets`. Post-vault attacks use `vault.landedAttack`. The mine's client distance calculation reads the gameplay attack and body rectangles.

Presentation `projectiles`, zombie `helm`, `shield` and `drops` define sprites, tracks, effects, offsets and sounds; sound `pitchRange` controls pitch randomization (negative ranges lower the pitch, as the newspaper rip's -2). An armor's `dropSounds` play with its `dropReanim` when it breaks, unless the hit bypasses the shield. Gameplay zombies have two armor slots as in the original: `helm` (`mHelmType`: cone, bucket) and `shield` (`mShieldType`: newspaper, screen door), each `{type, health}`. `Zombie::TakeDamage` hits the shield first, then the helm, then the body; a zombie may wear both. `seedPackets` owns atlas cells and placements; `almanac` owns cords and motion parameters. Add definitions for new content rather than branching on concrete IDs in shared renderers. View factories, dedicated ability state machines and unit conversion constants remain code.

Gameplay `boards.*.bounds` defines horizontal limits and zombie `dropsFlag` enables flag drops. Presentation `boards` defines backgrounds, music and game-over geometry. Particle snapshots retain optional remaining lifetimes; packet flashes use `seedPackets.flash`, with no effect-ID branches on restore. `mods/pvz-base/gameplay/store.json` owns shop tiers, requirements, inventory rules and purchase effects. The application loads that JSON and injects a catalog into the backend.

Mods use `register({ host, assets })`: `host.get()` returns host modules and registries; `host.around()` / `host.replace()` patch actual methods. Separate system, screen and animation enhancement facades have been removed. JSON ECS components registered in `GameplayRegistry.components` participate in world snapshots and saves. Server rules take the game `Session` first and are exported as objects: `server/planting` (seed availability, selection, planting, plant creation, cell lookup), `server/tools`, `server/items` (pickups), `server/commands` (`execute`, including `mod` commands) and `server/snapshot` (`build`). `IntegratedGameServer` only starts or restores the session, queues commands and advances ticks; `server/persistence` converts between the world and saves. See [the Mod capability example](../mods/example/README.md) and [Mod documentation](MODS.md).

Gameplay authoring tables live under `mods/pvz-base/gameplay/definitions/`, split into plants, zombies, seeds, boards, projectiles, items, lawn-mowers and zombie-gaits. `gameplay/settings.json` contains the global defaults, and the root `content.json` indexes every table. Packing merges them into `assets/gameplay.json` using the same structure as Mod packages; headless tools and tests read the same source tables.
